## Clouds

| Cloud | Looks like | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Cirrus** | High, thin, wispy "mares' tails" | Fair now; a front may arrive in 24–36 hours if they thicken |
| **Cirrostratus** | High thin veil, halo around sun or moon | Rain or snow likely within 12–24 hours |
| **Altocumulus** | Mid-level rows of puffy "mackerel sky" | Changeable; "mackerel sky and mares' tails make tall ships carry low sails" |
| **Altostratus** | Grey sheet, sun shows as through frosted glass | Rain likely within hours |
| **Nimbostratus** | Low, dark, featureless | Steady rain or snow now |
| **Cumulus** | Fluffy white with flat bases | Fair weather — if they stay small |
| **Cumulonimbus** | Towering, anvil top | Thunderstorms, hail, strong gusts, possible tornadoes. Seek shelter |
| **Stratus** | Low grey blanket, fog | Drizzle, dull weather |

## Signs of change

- **Red sky at night, shepherd's delight; red sky in the morning, shepherd's warning** — generally true where weather comes from the west.
- **Halo around the sun or moon**: moisture high up — rain or snow coming.
- **Wind backing** (changing anticlockwise, e.g. from west to south) in the northern hemisphere often means worsening weather; **veering** (clockwise) usually means clearing after a front.
- **Rising pressure** = improving weather; falling = worsening. Rapid fall = storm. A mechanical barometer (or many phones) shows this.
- Smells are stronger, sounds carry further, and smoke hangs low before rain.
- Morning dew or frost on grass: dry day likely. No dew on a clear night: wind or rain on the way.
- Swallows and swifts flying high: fair. Flying low: rain coming.
- Distant objects look unusually sharp and close: rain soon.

## Thunderstorms

Count seconds between lightning flash and thunder: **divide by 3 for km** (5 for miles). Under 30 seconds (10 km) = within striking range. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going out.

## Wind chill and heat

Wind makes cold air feel much colder: at 0 °C, a 30 km/h wind feels like –6 °C. High humidity makes heat more dangerous because sweat can't evaporate.
