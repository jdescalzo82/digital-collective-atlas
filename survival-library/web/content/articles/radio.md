When cell towers and internet fail, **radio still works** — person to person, with no infrastructure.

## Listen first

A battery, solar or hand-crank **AM/FM/shortwave receiver** gets official broadcasts and news. AM travels further at night. Shortwave reaches across continents. Keep spare batteries; listen at set times to save power.

## Two-way radio types

| Type | Range (typical) | Licence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FRS / PMR446** walkie-talkies | 0.5–3 km | None (FRS in US, PMR446 in Europe) | Cheap, widely owned. Channel numbers differ between brands — agree frequencies, not just channel numbers |
| **GMRS** (US) | 2–40 km with repeaters | Simple licence, no exam | Repeaters extend range a lot |
| **CB radio** (27 MHz) | 5–30 km; much further sometimes | None in most countries | Trucks, rural areas. **Channel 9 = emergency**, Channel 19 = road/traffic (US) |
| **Marine VHF** | 10–40 km over water | Required (normally) | **Channel 16 (156.8 MHz)** = distress, monitored by coast guards and ships |
| **Amateur (ham) radio** | Local to worldwide | Licence + exam | Organised emergency networks (ARES, RAYNET, etc.). Most flexible and powerful |
| **Aviation VHF** | Line of sight to aircraft | Restricted | **121.5 MHz** = aviation distress, monitored by many aircraft |

In a genuine life-threatening emergency, international law allows anyone to use any radio frequency to call for help.

## Common amateur radio calling frequencies

- **146.520 MHz** FM (2 m band, Americas national simplex calling)
- **145.500 MHz** FM (2 m calling, Europe/UK)
- **446.000 MHz** FM (70 cm calling, Americas)
- **7.110 / 7.060 MHz** and **14.300 MHz** — HF emergency/traffic nets in various regions

## Making a distress call (voice)

1. "**MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY**" (life in immediate danger) or "**PAN-PAN** ×3" (urgent but not life-threatening).
2. "This is [name/call sign/vessel]" ×3.
3. **Where** you are (coordinates from the Maps page, or landmarks).
4. **What** happened and what help you need.
5. **How many** people and their condition.
6. "Over." Listen, repeat at intervals.

## Getting more range

- **Height beats power.** Climb a hill, a roof, a tree. Every metre of height helps VHF/UHF radios.
- Hold the radio vertical, antenna clear of your body.
- A longer or external antenna mounted high makes the biggest difference.
- Talk briefly — saves batteries and lets others use the channel.

## Organise a community net

- Agree a **schedule**: e.g. on the hour, every 3 hours, for 10 minutes, channel X.
- One person acts as **net control**: calls each station in turn for check-ins, needs, news.
- Write down messages: time, from, to, content.
- Use plain language. Spell names with the phonetic alphabet: Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.
- Numbers: "wun, too, tree, fower, fife, six, seven, ait, niner, zero".
